jack-jack

jack-jack (짹짹) is a crow who came into our lives on a random day in May. My parents were on their way to Costco, on another of their errands, when they happened to spot a small crow, hunched over on the grass. They didn’t think much of it. They went on with their day.

On the drive back, the sun was setting, and my dad said “Let’s check if that crow is still there, just in case.” To their surprise, it was still on the grass, hunched over, with its head weighing heavily and its beak almost pushed into the mud.

They couldn’t leave him to get eaten by a coyote overnight. And so, they brought a cardboard box and blanket, and gloves to pick up the bird. It didn’t budge while my mom scooped it gently into the box. A horde of crows flew in a wild circle overhead, cawing as if in protest of the humans taking away one of their own. My mom yelled up at them “What are we supposed to do? We’re sorry! I’m sorry.”

None of the bird sanctuaries would take him. We looked up a wild animal veterinarian and drove 50 minutes to see her. The vet plucked out maggots, prescribed antibiotics, and recommended canned kitten food. We took him home.

⠂⠄⠄⠂⠁⠁⠂⠄⠄⠂⠁⠁⠂⠄⠄⠂ ⠂⠄⠄⠂☆

짹짹 (jack-jack) is the sound a bird makes in Korean.

A bird says 짹짹 when it sings, or when it calls its friends, or when it sounds an alarm.

Once we started feeding 짹짹 the antibiotics and the canned kitten food, he slowly recovered and came back to life. Whenever he sensed our presence, he opened his beak as wide as he could and SQUAWKED until we shoved food down his throat with a Q-tip.

⠂⠄⠄⠂⠁⠁⠂⠄⠄⠂⠁⠁⠂⠄⠄⠂ ⠂⠄⠄⠂☆

The plan was to keep him outside. But he would spend his whole day in front of our door, at a loss of where else to go. Whenever we opened the door, he would try to march right in. We eventually gave up and let him inside.

⠂⠄⠄⠂⠁⠁⠂⠄⠄⠂⠁⠁⠂⠄⠄⠂ ⠂⠄⠄⠂☆

짹짹 discovered that we have a rowing machine in our living room, with a long horizontal perch. At 7 PM every night, he would march over to the rowing machine, hop up, tuck his head into his wing, and fall asleep.

He did this every single day.

I was amazed.

⠂⠄⠄⠂⠁⠁⠂⠄⠄⠂⠁⠁⠂⠄⠄⠂ ⠂⠄⠄⠂☆

We learned that 짹짹 really loves sticks! I got him a long branch from a tree outside, and I showed it to him. He chirped and hopped up onto it. He didn’t want to leave the stick. To take him anywhere, all I had to do was present him with a good stick.

Years later, I saw a Stick Library for dogs. I thought about how 짹짹 would love that too.

⠂⠄⠄⠂⠁⠁⠂⠄⠄⠂⠁⠁⠂⠄⠄⠂ ⠂⠄⠄⠂☆

짹짹 had the prettiest blue eyes. He would close one eye, and peek at us with the other.

Baby corvids have blue eyes, which turn gray as they get older.

One day, I looked at 짹짹 and noticed that he had light gray eyes.

⠂⠄⠄⠂⠁⠁⠂⠄⠄⠂⠁⠁⠂⠄⠄⠂ ⠂⠄⠄⠂☆

짹짹 had avian flu. He had blisters that swelled up onto his face and beak, which eventually turned into bumpy scabs. We kept him away from other birds, and the other birds stayed away from him. So, he would follow us around the kitchen and living room all day, chirping and squawking, not knowing he looked so ugly.

⠂⠄⠄⠂⠁⠁⠂⠄⠄⠂⠁⠁⠂⠄⠄⠂ ⠂⠄⠄⠂☆

One day, 짹짹 was out on the lawn with me. Two crows flew across the sky, cawing. He started chasing them, running on his short legs and stumbling through the grass. He only made it a few steps until they were gone.

Did you know that crows see a wider range of colors including UV light?

They might seem all black to us, but if you spot a crow in your backyard on a bright sunny day, and the sunshine hits their feathers in just the right way, you can see a slight glimmer of rainbow, like oil.

I learned that 짹짹 loves the color pink. He would start shrieking and flapping his wings whenever I showed him my pink highlighter. Watermelon cubes soon became his favorite food. Whenever we show him a flash of bright pink watermelon in a cup, he would flap his wings, screaming, and peck desperately at the watermelon bits.

One day, we were sitting outside in our backyard with 짹짹. There were bright pink flower petals scattered over our concrete floor, and 짹짹 picked up the flower petals piece by piece, letting each of them go to flutter in the wind.

⠂⠄⠄⠂⠁⠁⠂⠄⠄⠂⠁⠁⠂⠄⠄⠂ ⠂⠄⠄⠂☆

짹짹 was getting sick again. The antibiotics stopped working, and he was eating less and less. We placed him in a basket and covered the top with a blanket. We drove him to the vet. Usually, he would scream at me, but in the office filled with whimpering dogs and meowing cats, he stayed still and quiet. When I would check under the blanket, he would peer back up at me, squinting with his one eye.

⠂⠄⠄⠂⠁⠁⠂⠄⠄⠂⠁⠁⠂⠄⠄⠂ ⠂⠄⠄⠂☆

We decided that for 짹짹 to eventually integrate into crow society, he’ll need to learn to fly. Every day, we would bring him a stick to perch onto, and then I’ll lift the stick into the air until 짹짹 started flapping his wings. He would usually only get to the other side of our yard before crashing into a table, but all of us were excited to see him gain more confidence.

One time, he flew over into our neighbor’s backyard. I climbed up the ladder to spot where he was, and saw him facing a dusty corner of the yard. My sister and I went to knock on our neighbor’s door - we explained that we had a crow who accidentally flew into her yard - could we go in and rescue him? She hesitated, confused, but let us in.

⠂⠄⠄⠂⠁⠁⠂⠄⠄⠂⠁⠁⠂⠄⠄⠂ ⠂⠄⠄⠂☆

My dad started eating his dinner outside on the patio table. 짹짹 would climb up onto his shoulders to get a better look, peering intensely with one of his eyes.

⠂⠄⠄⠂⠁⠁⠂⠄⠄⠂⠁⠁⠂⠄⠄⠂ ⠂⠄⠄⠂☆

짹짹 lived for 2 months with us.

In those 2 months,

I dreamed that 짹짹 would learn how to crack open nuts and seeds.

I dreamed that 짹짹 would catch his own bugs to eat.

I dreamed that 짹짹 would have a deep, strong caw like all the other crows. He’ll caw at other crows, and they’ll caw right back at him.

I dreamed that 짹짹 would learn how to fly long distances. We’ll see him fly away for a day or two, but he’ll come back and visit us.

I dreamed that 짹짹’s scabs would pop off one by one, leaving behind beautiful new black feathers.

I dreamed that 짹짹 would go on long journeys, but proudly return home with trinkets.

I dreamed that one day, I’ll look up at the sky and see 짹짹 flying with all the other crows, in a pack.

⠂⠄⠄⠂⠁⠁⠂⠄⠄⠂⠁⠁⠂⠄⠄⠂ ⠂⠄⠄⠂☆

Like every night, 짹짹 marched over to his rowing machine to perch and sleep. I came back late and when I went to say good night to him, he was still up.

The next morning, my mom woke me and told me that today might be 짹짹’s last day.

I ran downstairs and saw that he was in our living room, in his usual spot in front of our door. He didn’t take any of the water or watermelon juice we gave him. We all sat there with him for a few hours, at a loss of what to do.

짹짹 lost the strength to perch, and dropped to the floor. He lay there weakly; and when he breathed his last breath, my dad said in a broken voice: "잘가 짹짹아" “Farewell, our JackJack”

I put together a small wooden box of his favorite things.

  • His favorite twigs
  • The neon pink highlighter that would make him go crazy
  • His favorite seeds and snacks

I wrote him a letter, which I buried with him.

짹짹 was a baby crow who came into our lives on a random summer day in May.

The way my dad put it, our house was a resting place, to discover life and to learn love, before crossing the rainbow bridge.

We spent 2 whole months with him - every day a new surprise at how much love, and hope, and dreaming we had for one baby crow.
